# Research and analysis tasks

Read this before writing the brief for a research task; the evidence review before research conclusions are recorded is here too. Research tasks are work whose conclusions rest on experiments and data, such as data analysis, comparison experiments, model evaluation and performance investigations. The risk here isn't buggy code; it's conclusions that go beyond the evidence.

## What the brief adds
When handing research off, the brief must cover the items below on top of the general structure in `references/run.md`. If an item doesn't apply, say so and why instead of leaving it out. Questions and hypotheses already settled in a discussion go straight in; don't discuss them again.
- **Questions and hypotheses**: the questions to answer, and each hypothesis with the criteria for judging it. Fix the criteria in advance; don't change them after seeing results.
- **Controls**: which controls and baselines are needed, and what each one answers.
- **Robustness**: how to check that a result isn't a fluke of one parameter point, one batch of data or one period, for example parameter neighborhoods, another batch of data or time period, different random seeds or starting points.
- **Confounders**: list what could distort the conclusions, and require each to be ruled out or explained as not applicable. Common ones: scale and path dependence (results that shift with scale, the starting point or the order of steps), sample exposure (choosing after seeing results), multiple testing, survivorship bias, changes in how data is defined.
- **Statistics**: how to judge whether a difference is significant, and how to correct for comparing many groups.
- **Deliverables**: the evidence behind each conclusion (files, numbers, a way to recompute it), and anything that couldn't be verified.

## Evidence review before conclusions are recorded
Before research conclusions are put to use, level the evidence review by which decision would go wrong if they're wrong and how much that would cost, not by whether they're positive or negative or where they're written down. Level them this way whoever did the research:
- **Level 0**: conclusions that are cheap to get wrong: no decision rests on them yet (interim results, working notes, findings kept for later reference), or they only feed decisions whose mistakes would show up soon and are easy to undo (such as which approach to try in the next round). Claude checks each one against the evidence itself, with no outside review. When a costlier decision is later going to rest on such a conclusion, review it at level 2 or 3 as below before the decision is made.
- **Level 2**: ordinary research conclusions that wouldn't be cheap to get wrong, such as choosing the approach to adopt, deciding whether to keep investing, or handing the user a settled conclusion or writing it into a report for others to act on. A step inside the research that can't be redone doesn't change outside state, so it isn't an external action that can't be undone.
- **Level 3**: conclusions that will be used for decisions about money, deleting or migrating data, auth and security, or external actions that can't be undone, or where being wrong would do equally serious harm.
- Review together, in one review, the conclusions that one research stage is putting to use; when the research code has already been reviewed, the evidence review covers only the conclusions and the evidence.

Levels 2 and 3 use `review --target claims`; every seat is a new session, never the one that did the research, as `references/review.md` describes: the review brief lists each conclusion with where its evidence is and the key numbers, and the evidence files go in with `--file`; the reviewers judge each one: is the evidence enough, does it overstate, are there uncontrolled confounders, does it hold up statistically. The number of seats is the `seats` configured for that review level; if the user wants evidence reviews to use every model, set that level's `review.seats` to the number of candidates in the cell. Claude checks each finding as in "After the review" in review.md and corrects the conclusions before recording them. If a conclusion already recorded is overturned, correct it with an appended revision rather than editing the original.
